Mobile shooter sensitivity explained: camera, ADS, scope and gyro

Every type of sensitivity in mobile shooters explained, and a step-by-step method to find your own settings instead of copying someone else's codes.

By Zibno15 November 20264 min read

Sensitivity menus in mobile shooters can have dozens of sliders: camera, ADS, every scope, firing, gyro, vehicles. Copying a pro's numbers rarely works, because they were set for a different phone, screen size and grip. This guide explains what each type of sensitivity does and gives you a simple method to find settings that fit your hands.

The types of sensitivity

Camera (look) sensitivity
How fast your view turns when you swipe while not aiming. Controls looking around, checking corners and turning to face someone.
ADS sensitivity
How fast your view turns when you swipe while aiming down sights. Usually set per scope: red dot, 2x, 3x, 4x, 6x, 8x.
Firing sensitivity
Some games have a separate value while the fire button is held. Lower values make recoil easier to control with your thumb.
Gyroscope sensitivity
How far the camera turns when you tilt the phone. Usually also set per scope. See the gyroscope guide.
Vehicle sensitivity
Camera speed while driving or flying. Delta Force has its own vehicle sensitivity settings, see Delta Force Mobile settings in English.

Why every scope has its own setting

A higher zoom makes every movement look bigger on screen. If an 8x scope used the same sensitivity as a red dot, the crosshair would fly across the target with the smallest swipe. So scopes with more zoom need lower sensitivity values to feel similar. The goal is for every scope to feel equally controllable, not for the numbers to match.

A step-by-step method to find your sensitivity

Do this in the training ground, one setting at a time, starting from the game's default values.

  1. Camera sensitivity: swipe from one side of the screen to the other in one comfortable motion. Aim for a turn of roughly 180 degrees. If you turn too far, lower it; if you need two swipes, raise it.
  2. Red dot ADS: aim at a target and strafe left and right, keeping the dot on its chest. If you overshoot, lower it. If you fall behind, raise it.
  3. Higher scopes: repeat on targets further away, adjusting each scope so it feels like your red dot.
  4. Firing sensitivity: spray a rifle at a wall at medium range. If your thumb cannot pull the gun down fast enough, raise it slightly; if the spray jumps around, lower it.
  5. Play a few real matches, then fine-tune. Training targets do not move like real players.

Tip Change values in small steps, about 5 to 10 percent at a time, and only one slider before testing again. Big jumps make it impossible to tell what helped.

Why copied sensitivity codes rarely work

  • Screen size: the same value feels faster on a small phone and slower on a tablet, because your thumb covers a different distance.
  • Grip and number of fingers: a 4-finger claw player swipes differently from a thumbs player. See finger layouts explained.
  • Gyro or no gyro: gyro players often use lower swipe sensitivity, because gyro does the fine aim.
  • Frame rate: the same settings can feel different at 60 and 120 FPS.

A shared code is fine as a starting point. Then run the method above to adjust it to you.

Signs your sensitivity is wrong

Too high
You overshoot targets, your aim shakes, and you keep correcting back and forth.
Too low
You fall behind moving players, need several swipes to turn, and lose close fights to people who strafe.
Scopes unbalanced
One scope feels fine and another feels slippery or heavy. Adjust the odd one until it matches.

Keep it consistent

  • Write down or screenshot your values, so you can restore them after an update or on a new phone.
  • Use the game's cloud save or sensitivity code feature if it has one.
  • Once it feels good, stop changing it. Muscle memory needs the same settings for weeks, not days.
  • Re-test only when something changes: a new phone, a new layout, or switching to gyro.

FAQ

What is ADS sensitivity?

The speed your view turns when you swipe while aiming down sights. Most games let you set it separately for each scope.

Should higher scopes have lower sensitivity?

Usually yes. Higher zoom magnifies every movement, so lower values make each scope feel equally controllable.

Should I copy a pro player's sensitivity?

Use it as a starting point only. Their phone, screen size and grip are different, so adjust it with the method in this guide.

How often should I change my sensitivity?

Rarely. Find settings that feel good, then keep them so your muscle memory can build.
